What to compare before you contact anyone

A family law enquiry is easier when you know what you are comparing. The directory format is useful because it puts reviews, photos and service comparisons in one place, which reduces guesswork. Instead of asking every firm the same basic questions from scratch, you can use profile information to decide who merits a first conversation. Start with service fit. Some firms present themselves broadly, while others frame their work more tightly. Then check whether the profile gives enough detail to understand how the practice works, because a sparse listing may mean you need more screening questions before you invest time in a call. Reviews can help here, but they are most useful when read alongside the service description rather than in isolation. Does the profile clearly explain the type of legal help offered? Are reviews recent enough to be useful for your decision? Can you see enough detail to compare one provider with another? Is there a direct contact path, such as a quote request form, that suits how you want to start?

Use the first enquiry to test process

Once you have a shortlist, the first contact should test how the firm communicates and whether the listing gave you an accurate picture. That gives you a simple way to compare responsiveness across providers without overcomplicating the first step. Ask questions that clarify process. You are not trying to solve the legal issue in the first message.
